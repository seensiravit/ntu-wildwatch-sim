Two-Way Traffic Study

Which setting cuts serious hits for the least delay to drivers? The earlier studies drove one car at a time. This one runs a stream of cars in both directions on the same two copies of the road (without and with the system, same animals), with cars following and queuing behind each other, and measures what the system costs drivers in waiting and queues as well as what it saves in hits.

This is the first two-way pass, at the presentation demo's settings and before a car-following bug was fixed. The Installation Study repeats it with research values and the fixed model; use that one for decisions. This page is kept because it introduced the flow measures and the stopping-time comparison.

What this study found

Words used in this report

Serious hit
a car touches an animal while going faster than 30 km/h. Counted per 100 animals that walked onto the road.
Delay
how much longer a car takes to cross the stretch than it would on an empty road, including any wait to get onto the road behind a queue.
Slowest 5% (p95)
the delay that only one car in twenty exceeds.
Driver-seconds per hit
all the extra waiting, added up over every driver, divided by the serious hits prevented. The price of each hit avoided.
Unneeded
the share of the time the sign is on while no animal is on the road (it saw one on the grass).

How the study worked

  • Each run is the demo itself with the drawing switched off, for 20 simulated minutes: two copies of the road share the same arriving animals; one has no camera or signs, the other has today's camera and signs.
  • Cars arrive from both ends at random, about one every 20 s in each direction (180 cars an hour each way), each with its own speed (50 ± 4 km/h). A car gets onto the road only when there is room; otherwise it waits and the wait counts. Cars keep a 2 m plus 1.5 s gap, notice the car ahead braking after 0.5 s, and cannot see an animal hidden behind it. Every other driver rule is the demo's.
  • Westbound cars drive the south lane right to left and obey the same two signs. An animal hit in the second lane it entered is a far-lane hit, something one-way studies cannot see.
  • One setting at a time from today's settings, 300 seeds each (the same seeds for all), so differences come from the setting. The best level of each system setting was then run together with the baseline on 1,000 seeds. The camera placement grid was re-run two-way with 100 seeds and one sign per direction.

What they buy and cost

    How busy the road is

    The same settings at three traffic levels. The safety cut holds; the delay and the queue grow with traffic because every car behind a waiting car waits too. At a car every 10 s the worst queue reached 22 cars in 20 minutes.

    Reading it

      Running the best levels together (1,000 seeds)

      The best level of each system setting by serious hits, run together against the baseline on the same seeds. It is the safest configuration tried, and the slowest: it buys 0.56 fewer serious hits per 100 crossings for 9.6 s more delay per car.

      Recommendation (from this pass)

      Recommended layout

      Seen from above; 1 map unit is about 14 cm. One thermal camera on the centreline just east of the hotspot (x 590), looking west with a 110° lens and 35 m range, so it sees both verges and both lanes of the crossing. Two signs at the camera, one each side of the road, facing both directions. Eastbound drivers stop at a line 24 m before the hotspot centre; westbound drivers at a line 17 m east of it. A warned driver waits at most 10 s after the sign stops flashing, then drives through slowly. The dashed camera is the optional second one on the Hall 15 side facing north.

      What this study cannot tell you

      • It uses the presentation demo's settings (fast animals, a quick driver, a 110° lens, three in four animals from the wildlife verge), and its car-following model still had a bug that made followers brake too hard. The Installation Study corrects all of this.
      • Random car arrivals suit light traffic; real cars arrive in bunches after junctions, so queues at a car every 10 s are a lower bound.
      • The following model is simple (no overtaking, no lane changes, the same reaction for everyone), and a queued driver cannot see past the car ahead.
      • The camera never misses an animal in its view; animal behaviour is modelled, not measured on Nanyang Crescent. The percentages compare settings; they do not predict real counts.
